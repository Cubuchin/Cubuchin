## Overview

CyberPsych with Connor is a public education project that explains how psychology, digital behavior, and cybersecurity interact. The goal is to make security incidents and behavioral concepts understandable without relying on fear, hacker clichés, or unnecessary technical jargon.

Topics include social engineering, scams, privacy, persuasion, cognitive bias, attention, decision fatigue, trust cues, reporting behavior, AI-assisted deception, and practical ways people can interrupt risky decisions.

## Editorial Approach

### Start with the event

Each breakdown begins with a concrete incident, scam pattern, technology change, or everyday digital behavior.

### Explain why it works

The content then examines the human factors that make the situation effective or confusing, such as urgency, authority, social proof, familiarity, cognitive load, divided attention, or misplaced trust.

### End with practical action

The final section gives the audience a manageable protective behavior, verification habit, reporting step, or decision rule they can use outside the video.

## Recurring Content Formats

### Cyberpsychology breakdowns

Current-event stories are translated into a clear sequence:

1. What happened
2. Why the story matters psychologically
3. What the audience can do differently

### Practical cyberpsychology

Standalone lessons focus on reusable concepts, including how people respond to pressure, why familiarity can feel like legitimacy, how decision fatigue changes security behavior, and how to build better verification habits.

### Short-form adaptations

Longer scripts can be condensed into brief, structured explanations that preserve the core event, psychological insight, and practical takeaway.
